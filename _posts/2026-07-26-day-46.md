what_i_learned: |
   Today marked the second and final day of the  National Symposium on Effective and Ethical AI, where all program participants presented the research projects they had been working on throughout the summer. Throughout the presentations, I had the opportunity to learn about the diverse AI research being conducted by my peers, ask questions, and gain a deeper understanding of how artificial intelligence can be applied across different fields. My team then presented our final research project, DermaBridge: A Trustworthy Multimodal AI App for Skin Lesion Screening, Explainable Risk Assessment, and Smart Clinical Referral. During our presentation, we shared the complete journey of our project, from preparing and preprocessing our datasets to training and evaluating our machine learning models, developing the DermaBridge application, and writing our final research paper. It was rewarding to showcase the progress we had made over the course of the program and answer questions from the audience about our research and application. In addition to presenting my research project, I also had the opportunity to present our hackathon project, Class Radar, where we introduced our real-time classroom feedback application designed to improve communication between teachers and students. Following the presentations, the symposium concluded with an awards ceremony, where my team was recognized with third place for our project pitch video, while my graduate mentor, Ms. Blessing Adeika, received first place for our research poster presentation. Seeing both our team's and my mentor's hard work recognized made the celebration especially meaningful and served as a rewarding conclusion to the summer. The event concluded with a farewell dinner and closing celebration, giving everyone an opportunity to reflect on the relationships, knowledge, and experiences we gained throughout the SAIRI program.
