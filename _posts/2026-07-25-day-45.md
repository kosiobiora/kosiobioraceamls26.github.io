what_i_learned: |
   Today marked the first day of the SAIRI Final Symposium, beginning with participant registration followed by the opening remarks that officially welcomed everyone to the event. After the welcome session, we attended an insightful keynote presentation on Equitable AI, which emphasized the importance of developing artificial intelligence systems that are fair and accessible. Following the keynote, all SAIRI participants gathered for a group photo to commemorate the start of the symposium before exploring the various research sessions and workshops taking place throughout the day. As I visited the different sessions, I had the opportunity to learn about a wide range of AI research projects and gain new perspectives from both students and professionals in the field. I also made it a priority to attend the poster presentation session to support my graduate mentor, Ms. Blessing Adeika, as she presented our team's research poster on our behalf. It was rewarding to see our project showcased during the symposium and to observe attendees engaging with the research through thoughtful questions and discussions. Later in the day, I registered for the symposium hackathon, where participants were challenged to develop a solution for K–12 students that promotes meaningful human-AI interaction. I teamed up with Ayomide and Stephanie, and together we spent time identifying a real classroom problem before brainstorming several possible solutions. After evaluating our ideas, we decided to build Class Radar, a real-time classroom feedback application designed to help teachers better understand student comprehension while encouraging students to communicate their confusion more comfortably. We concluded the day by planning the app's core features, selecting the development tools we would use, and beginning the initial stages of building our prototype.
